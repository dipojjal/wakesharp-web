The alarm went off at 6:30. You know it did, because the phone says it was dismissed at 6:31. You have no memory of that minute. If this is you most mornings, you are a heavy sleeper, and the problem is not the volume. It is that the part of you that silences an alarm is awake a long time before the part of you that makes decisions, a gap we covered in [why you sleep through alarms you do not remember dismissing](/blog/sleep-through-alarm-dismissed-no-memory).

So the question for an alarm app is not "how loud is it?" but "what does it ask of me before the morning counts?" Below are six apps worth considering, plus your phone's own clock as the baseline. One of them, WakeSharp, is ours. We have tried to hold it to the same standard as the rest, and where another app does something better, we say so.

## What a heavy sleeper actually needs from an alarm app

1. **A task a half-asleep hand cannot do.** Swiping is a reflex. Solving arithmetic, finding an object across the room or answering out loud needs you to be conscious for a moment, which is the whole idea behind a [math alarm clock](/blog/math-alarm-science-solving-beats-snoozing).
2. **It rings when the phone is on silent.** How an app schedules its alarm decides whether it survives Silent mode, Focus or Do Not Disturb. The details differ by platform, and [we went through them here](/blog/will-alarm-ring-on-silent-dnd).
3. **It survives the battery manager.** On Android, a phone maker's battery optimizer can quietly stop an alarm app from ringing at all, which is the most common reason [an Android alarm does not go off](/blog/android-alarm-not-ringing-battery-optimizer).
4. **Snoozing does not feel free.** A snooze button with no cost trains you to use it. The research on [what snoozing actually costs](/blog/snooze-button-psychology-cost) is gentler than its reputation, but an alarm that asks for nothing will get nothing.
5. **Honest limits.** No app can ring on a phone that is switched off, and none can promise you will get up. Be wary of any listing that says otherwise.

## How we compared them

Everything below about the other apps comes from their own US App Store listings (and, for Alarmy, its own website), read on September 24, 2026. Prices are what the listings show in US dollars; your country's store may differ, and all of these can change. We did not test every feature of every app, so this is a comparison of what each one says it does, which is the honest way to put it.

### Your phone's built-in alarm

The Clock app on iPhone and the Clock app on Android cost nothing, show no ads and are the most reliable way to make a sound at a time, because the operating system treats them as first-class alarms. On iPhone, a Clock alarm rings through Silent mode and Focus. What they do not do is ask anything of you: one tap stops them, and the default snooze is 9 minutes on iPhone and 10 in Google's Clock. For a light sleeper that is enough. For a heavy one, it is the problem this whole category exists to solve. Keep it anyway, as your independent backup for mornings that matter.

### Alarmy

Alarmy is the best-known app here and it has the ratings to show for it: 4.8 on the US App Store from more than 247,000 ratings. Its website lists eight mission types: math, a memory game, shaking the phone, typing, squats, QR and barcode scanning, walking and a photo. It also includes a sleep tracker that records snoring, sleep sounds and bedtime reminders, and its listing describes features it calls Power Off Prevention and Anti-Snooze.

The catch is the business model. Alarmy is free to download, its site says most features work without paying, and the App Store listing says it contains advertising. Its in-app purchases are listed from $4.99 up to $69.99. **Best for:** anyone who wants to start without paying, wants sleep and snore tracking in the same app, or likes the idea of squats at 6am.

### Wayk

Wayk leans into real-world tasks. Its listing gives examples like doing pushups, taking a photo of the sky, making your bed, finding a random object, or reading an affirmation or a Bible verse aloud, and says the alarm stops only once the mission is done. It is rated 4.7 from more than 19,000 ratings. The listing does not say the app contains advertising; it lists a monthly plan at $9.99 and yearly plans from $19.99. **Best for:** people who want the morning task to double as a habit, such as a made bed or a short workout.

### SuperAlarm

SuperAlarm lists six missions: math problems, memory games, barcode scanning, object scanning, Face ID and walking. It adds a feature it calls Wake up check, which the listing says is there so you do not drift off again. It is rated 4.6 from about 5,000 ratings. The listing says it contains advertising, and its in-app purchases range from weekly subscriptions around $4.49 to lifetime options up to $69.99, with a separate ad removal at $2.99 a month. **Best for:** people who want the Wake up check and do not mind choosing between several ways to pay.

### Sleep Cycle

Sleep Cycle is a different kind of app, and it is here because heavy sleepers ask about it. It is a sleep tracker first: it listens to your night through the microphone (or uses motion), detects snoring, and its Smart Alarm, in the listing's words, wakes you "during your lightest sleep phase". There are no missions. It is rated 4.7 from about 29,000 ratings, the listing does not mention advertising, and premium options range from $2.99 to $57.99. **Best for:** people whose problem is grogginess rather than sleeping through the alarm, and who want to track their nights. If you routinely dismiss alarms in your sleep, a gentler wake-up will not fix that on its own.

### Loud Alarm Clock (the LOUDEST)

This one does what its name says. The listing promises volume-boosted sounds, a random sound each morning so you cannot get used to it, and a time after which you can no longer snooze. It is rated 4.8 from about 39,000 ratings, the listing does not mention advertising, and it offers a single in-app purchase at $7.99. **Best for:** people who genuinely do not hear a normal alarm. It will not help if you hear it perfectly well and switch it off on autopilot, because loudness is not a task.

### WakeSharp (ours)

WakeSharp is an [alarm clock for heavy sleepers](/) that asks for a mission instead of a swipe. The missions come in several kinds: arithmetic, memory, sequence, colour and typing puzzles, a photo of a spot you chose the night before, a real object across the room, steps, daylight at a window, or an answer said out loud, and an alarm can ask for several in a row. However you quiet the alarm, the morning only counts once the mission is done. Afterwards, an optional two-minute warm-up gives you a Sharpness Score, an in-app score measured against your own baseline.

Two things none of the other listings here mention. Its alarm reliability check reads the settings on your phone that actually stop alarms (permissions, alarm volume, notifications, battery restrictions) and gives a verdict: the alarm will ring, it may not, or it cannot. And its smart alarms can follow your calendar, so the alarm can move when your first meeting does, with the calendar read on the phone. It shows no ads. It is sold as a subscription, WakeSharp Unlimited: a 7-day free trial of the yearly plan, then $34.99 a year, or $4.99 a month with no trial.

What it does not do: it has no sleep tracking of any kind, no squats or shaking, and as a new app it has only a handful of ratings so far. If those matter to you, Alarmy or Sleep Cycle will suit you better. **Best for:** heavy sleepers who want no ads, want to know the alarm will ring before they go to sleep, or whose wake time depends on their calendar.

## How to choose

- **You sleep through alarms you never remember hearing:** you need a mission, not more volume. Alarmy, Wayk, SuperAlarm and WakeSharp all ask for one; pick by the kind of task you will actually do half-asleep.
- **You do not hear the alarm at all:** start with a louder sound, then add a mission once you are waking up to it.
- **You wake up but feel terrible:** that is [sleep inertia](/blog/sleep-inertia-why-you-wake-up-groggy), and a tracker with a gentle wake-up window, a steadier bedtime or both will help more than any mission.
- **Whatever you choose:** check tonight that it can ring, using our [six-point checklist](/blog/alarm-reliability-checklist-tonight), and keep your phone's own alarm as a backup for the mornings that matter.

## FAQ

### What is the best alarm app for heavy sleepers?

The one that asks for a task you cannot do on autopilot and that you will not disable in week two. For most heavy sleepers that means a mission alarm: Alarmy if you want to start without paying and like physical tasks, Wayk for habit-style tasks, and WakeSharp if you want no ads, a reliability check and calendar-aware alarms. Keep your phone's built-in alarm as a backup either way.

### Do alarm apps still ring when the phone is on silent?

It depends on how the app schedules the alarm and on the platform. On iPhone, an app that uses Apple's AlarmKit rings through Silent mode and Focus once you grant it alarm permission. On Android, an alarm played on the alarm stream rings through silent mode, and through Do Not Disturb when it allows alarms. The full breakdown is in [will your alarm ring on silent](/blog/will-alarm-ring-on-silent-dnd).

### Are free alarm apps with ads worse?

Not automatically. Ads pay for a free download, and Alarmy has built a very popular app that way. The trade-off is that the first screen you see after a mission may be an advert, and some of the features that matter most to heavy sleepers sit behind premium. Whether that is worth it is a personal call.

### Can an alarm app guarantee I will wake up?

No, and be wary of any that says it can. A phone that is switched off or out of battery cannot ring, your phone's own controls always work, and a battery optimizer can stop an app on some Android phones. For anything you cannot afford to miss, use a second, independent alarm.
