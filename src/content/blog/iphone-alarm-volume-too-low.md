Yesterday at 2:15, in a meeting that would not end, your phone chimed twice in your pocket. You pressed volume-down until it ran out of clicks, mouthed an apology, and forgot about it.

At 6:30 this morning your alarm went off at exactly that level: a thin, polite chime from the nightstand, about as loud as a microwave finishing two rooms away. You woke at 7:12, to the garbage truck.

Nothing was broken. Your iPhone did precisely what it was told; it was just told by the person in that meeting, not the one asleep at 6:30.

**The short answer:** by default, an iPhone alarm plays at your ringer volume, not the media volume you hear in music and videos. On iOS 27, open Settings, then Sounds & Haptics, turn off Match Ringtone Volume under Alarms and Timers, and drag that slider up; on iOS 26, raise the slider under Ringtone and Alerts on the same screen. Then turn off Change with Buttons so a stray press cannot lower it, and give it a 30-second test from your pillow.

If your alarm made no sound at all, that is a different problem with different suspects, covered in [iPhone alarm not going off? The 7 real causes](/blog/iphone-alarm-didnt-go-off-causes). This one is about the alarm that rang and lost.

## Why your alarm follows your ringer

Apple's guide is precise about the side buttons: with Change with Buttons on, they adjust media while audio is playing and the ringer and alerts the rest of the time ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/adjust-the-volume-iphb71f9b54d/27/ios/27)). Alarms ride on that ringer volume: through iOS 26 they share one slider with it, and on iOS 27 alarms, timers, alerts and system sounds still match your ringtone volume by default ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-sounds-and-vibrations-iph07c867f28/27/ios/27)).

So every press of a volume button while nothing is playing is also an alarm setting, though it never looks like one. iOS 27 finally adds a switch to cut that link.

## How to change your iPhone alarm volume

### On iOS 27: give alarms their own slider

1. Open Settings and tap Sounds & Haptics.
2. Under Alarms and Timers, turn off Match Ringtone Volume.
3. Drag its volume slider to the level you want. Heavy sleepers: the top.

That is the whole change ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/27/ios/27)): turning the ringer down no longer takes the alarm with it. Timers share it, so the pasta timer will ring as loudly as your alarm. Alerts and System Sounds has its own switch, so notifications can stay discreet while the alarm stays loud.

### On iOS 26 and earlier: raise the shared slider

If Sounds & Haptics has no Alarms and Timers section, you are on iOS 26 or earlier, and one slider does everything. On iOS 26, drag the slider under Ringtone and Alerts ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/26/ios/26)), which Apple says sets the volume for all sounds ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-sounds-and-vibrations-iph07c867f28/26/ios/26)); iOS 18 labels it Ringtone and Alert Volume ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/18/ios/18)). An alert plays as you drag, so you hear the level as you set it ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118444)).

### On any version: lock it

On the same screen, turn off Change with Buttons. Apple's own tip is that this switch is what lets the buttons change the alarm volume; with it off, they control media only. You lose a thumb-sized shortcut and gain an alarm that is set once, on purpose.

## Loud enough awake is not loud enough asleep

A level that sounds startling at 11 PM is being judged by a brain that is awake. The one that has to hear it at 6:30 is not, and how much sound it takes to wake a sleeper (the auditory arousal threshold) moves.

It rises after lost deep sleep. Ferrara and colleagues deprived ten men of deep sleep for two nights; on the recovery night, deep sleep rebounded and they needed a significantly louder tone to wake, even from lighter sleep ([Clinical Neurophysiology, 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10616120/)). One small lab study is a direction, not a dose, but an awkward one: the morning after a run of short nights, the one you can least afford to oversleep, is likely when your alarm needs the most headroom. Our [sleep debt calculator](/tools/sleep-debt-calculator) shows how short the week was.

It depends on the sound, too. Bruck and colleagues played nine signals to 39 young adults in deep sleep, raising the volume until each person woke. Low-pitched square-wave beeps, at 400 and 520 Hz, woke people at significantly lower volumes than pure tones, whoops or white noise ([Journal of Sleep Research, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19302343/)).

And it depends on the room: a fan or noise machine running all night is the sound your alarm has to beat, so a slider test in silence flatters it.

## Five more ways to make your iPhone alarm louder

### 1. Silence your day with Silent mode, not the slider

Most of us turn the ringer down to keep the phone quiet in public. Silent mode does that and leaves the alarm alone: Apple notes that Clock alarms still play through the built-in speaker when Silent mode is on, whether you set it in Control Center, with the Ring/Silent switch or with the Action button ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/silence-iphone-iph81c7fd7d1/27/ios/27)). The full rundown is in [will your alarm ring on silent or Do Not Disturb](/blog/will-alarm-ring-on-silent-dnd).

### 2. Choose a tone that cuts through

Sound is set alarm by alarm, in Clock, and tones previewed at the same volume do not all sound equally loud. Favor a hard, abrupt start over a melody that fades in, and a buzzy beep over a soft chime; in the deep-sleep study above, the square waves won. If you use a song, check how it opens, since plenty start quietly. And make sure the sound is not set to None, which only vibrates ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118444)).

### 3. Lift the speaker limits

Sounds & Haptics also has a Volume Limit that caps how loud anything plays through the built-in speaker: under Built-In Speaker on iOS 27, next to Reduce Loud Sounds, which softens loud sounds, and directly in Sounds & Haptics on iOS 26 ([Apple, iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/adjust-the-volume-iphb71f9b54d/27/ios/27); [iOS 26](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/adjust-the-volume-iphb71f9b54d/26/ios/26)). Apple does not single out alarms either way, so if either is on, turn it off and retest. On a child's iPhone, a parent can lock the limit through Screen Time.

### 4. Give the sound a way out

Apple's speaker troubleshooting includes taking off cases, films and screen protectors and cleaning the speaker opening with a small, clean, dry, soft-bristled brush. If the ringtone slider plays nothing as you drag it, or its speaker button is dimmed, the speaker may need service ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118432)). Overnight, keep it on a hard surface, speaker uncovered, and out from under your pillow: Apple's safety guidance says not to sleep on a phone, or put it under a pillow or blanket, while it is connected to power ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/important-safety-information-iph301fc905/27/ios/27)).

### 5. Add vibration as a backup, not a plan

The alarm's Sound screen is also where you choose its vibration ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/27/ios/27)). Have one, but do not lean on it: a phone on a nightstand mostly vibrates the nightstand, and in StandBy, alarm haptics are switched off ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118444)).

## Why does my iPhone alarm get quieter?

If it starts loud and drops when you look at the screen, suspect Attention Aware Features. On iPhones with Face ID, when the phone detects your attention, it lowers the volume of your alerts ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102216)). Apple does not list alarms specifically, but if yours dips when you face it, turn the feature off in Settings, then Face ID & Passcode.

A sleep schedule's wake up alarm also has its own volume slider, in Clock under Alarms, then Change, then Alarm Options ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-your-wake-up-alarm-iphf2a780f81/27/ios/27)). And with headphones connected, Apple says the alarm plays through both the headphones and the built-in speakers ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/27/ios/27)).

## The 30-second pillow test

A slider tests the alarm at arm's length, awake. What counts is how it sounds from your pillow.

1. In Clock, set an alarm for two minutes from now, with your real alarm's sound.
2. Lock the phone and put it where it sleeps: same spot, same case, on the charger.
3. Turn on whatever runs all night (fan, purifier, noise machine), and put in earplugs if you wear them.
4. Lie down, eyes closed, and listen for 30 seconds.

If you have to concentrate to hear it, it is too quiet. You want a sound it would be rude to sleep through. Repeat the test with any other alarm app you rely on.

## When louder is not the answer

One reason a loud, clean speaker still loses is hearing. About 15 percent of American adults report some trouble hearing, and about one in three people aged 65 to 74 has hearing loss; it arrives gradually, so it is easy to miss, and it makes phones and smoke alarms harder to hear ([NIDCD](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/health/age-related-hearing-loss)). In a study of 38 adults with mild to moderately severe hearing loss, woken from deep sleep, bed and pillow shakers woke 80 to 84 percent at their out-of-the-box setting. The kind of sound mattered too: at the same 75 decibels, a low-pitched 520 Hz beep woke 92 percent and the standard high-pitched smoke alarm 56 percent ([Ear and Hearing, 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19125029/)). If you suspect your hearing, see your doctor or an audiologist before reaching for a louder ringtone.

The other is hearing it and not waking. If you find the alarm dismissed with no memory of doing it, volume will not fix that; we took the mechanism apart in [why you sleep through alarms you don't remember dismissing](/blog/sleep-through-alarm-dismissed-no-memory). And if you regularly sleep through alarms that wake the rest of the house, or feel sleepy all day after a full night in bed, tell a doctor. Loud snoring, breathing that stops and starts, and gasping for air in your sleep are symptoms of sleep apnea, which a sleep study can help diagnose ([NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-apnea/symptoms)).

## Where WakeSharp fits

Here is what an alarm app like [WakeSharp](/download) can and cannot do about volume on iPhone.

It cannot play louder than iOS allows. On iPhone, system alarm volume is controlled by iOS, and Apple's developer support has said that AlarmKit, the Apple framework WakeSharp uses to schedule alarms, offers no volume control beyond the system volume ([Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/813519)). What AlarmKit does give it: once you grant alarm permission, a WakeSharp alarm on iPhone rings through Silent mode and Focus.

What it can change is the sound. The alarm editor has a [Loud category](/features/loud-alarm-clock) of tones for an alarm that stands out; preview them in the sound picker and choose one for each alarm. A Loud tone does not override your iPhone's alarm volume, so set the volume first, then give WakeSharp's alarm the pillow test.

And since no volume can promise you wake, a WakeSharp alarm ends with a mission: a few sums in Math Problems, steps counted by Walk It Off, or retaking the photo of a spot you chose the night before with Photo Proof. However you quiet the alarm, the morning only counts once the mission is done. Your phone's own controls always work: nothing stops you switching the phone off. A mission asks for more than a swipe. For a flight or an exam, set a second alarm on a separate device too.

## FAQ

### Why is my iPhone alarm volume so low?

Usually because it follows your ringer. By default an iPhone alarm plays at your ringtone volume, and with Change with Buttons on, the side buttons lower that volume whenever nothing is playing. On iOS 27, turn off Match Ringtone Volume under Alarms and Timers in Settings, then Sounds & Haptics, and raise the alarm's own slider; on iOS 26, raise Ringtone and Alerts on the same screen. If it is at the top and still weak, check Volume Limit and the speaker opening.

### Do the volume buttons change the alarm volume on iPhone?

They can. With Change with Buttons on, the buttons adjust the ringer whenever nothing is playing, and an alarm that matches your ringtone volume moves with it. Turn Change with Buttons off in Sounds & Haptics and the buttons only control media.

### How do I change the alarm sound on iPhone?

Open Clock, tap Alarms, then tap the alarm's time (or tap Edit, then the alarm). Tap Sound, choose a ringtone, a song or a vibration, and save. Each alarm keeps its own sound, so repeat it for each one.

### Where do I find my alarm settings on my iPhone?

In two places. Clock holds each alarm's time, repeat days, label, sound and snooze; Settings, then Sounds & Haptics, holds the volume, under Alarms and Timers on iOS 27 and under Ringtone and Alerts on iOS 26. A sleep schedule's wake up alarm keeps its own sound and volume, in Clock under Alarms, then Change.
