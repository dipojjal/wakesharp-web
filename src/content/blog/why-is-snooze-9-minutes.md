The alarm goes off at 6:30, and your hand finds Snooze before your eyes open. It goes off again at 6:39, and again at 6:48. By 6:57 you are sitting on the edge of the bed, and a question surfaces the way only useless questions do before coffee: why nine? Ten is a number people choose. Nine is a number somebody chose for you.

For most of the iPhone's life, there was nothing to choose: Apple's guide for iOS 18 mentions the nine minutes in passing, as a fact about Snooze rather than a setting ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/18/ios/18)). Since iOS 26, it is a setting.

**The short answer:** on iOS 26 and iOS 27, open Clock, tap Alarms, tap the alarm's time, make sure Snooze is on, then tap Snooze Duration and pick a length (when iOS 26 added it, the choices ran from 1 to 15 minutes, with 9 as the default). On iOS 18 and earlier there is no setting: Snooze pauses the alarm for 9 minutes. As for why nine, the documented history does not say. The first snooze alarm clock, in 1956, gave about 15 minutes, and the famous gear-teeth explanation is a legend.

## Why is the snooze button 9 minutes?

The first snooze alarm clock was General Electric-Telechron's Snooz-Alarm, model 7H241, in 1956 ([Clockhistory.com](https://clockhistory.com/westclox/products/electric/drowse/index.html)). Its advertising called it "the world's most humane alarm clock," and it was not a nine-minute clock: a February 1956 trade notice, reproduced on the collector site Telechron.net, describes about 15 minutes of quiet per press, and the site's owner timed his own early example at 16 to 17 minutes ([Telechron.net](https://telechron.net/eod/7h241.htm)). He adds that GE-Telechron later settled on a snooze gear of around nine minutes. Why nine, he does not say, and nothing from the period that we could find does either.

The competition did not agree on a number. Westclox's first snooze clock, the Drowse Electric of 1959, had a rocker switch for 5 or 10 minutes, and by 1969 its range included a Minikin Drowse with a 7-minute snooze and a Magic Touch Drowse with 10 ([Clockhistory.com](https://clockhistory.com/westclox/products/electric/drowse/index.html)). The snooze was born at about a quarter of an hour, and for decades it came in fives, sevens, nines and tens.

### The legend, labelled as legend

The most repeated explanation goes like this: the snooze had to fit an existing clock movement, the gear teeth allowed either just under ten minutes or just over, and the engineers chose nine because ten would let a sleeper slide back into deep sleep. A digital-age version says early clocks only had to watch the last digit of the minutes, so nine was the longest snooze that never touched the tens column. Both are tidy. We could not find a document, patent or named engineer behind either, and the record above already strains the first: GE's first snooze ran about 15 minutes, so nine was a revision, not the starting point.

What is documented is the habit. The iPhone kept nine until iOS 26 ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/09/30/ios-26-gave-your-iphones-alarm-brand-new-snooze-options/)), and people still snooze in roughly that unit: the average gap between alarms was 8 minutes among 1,195 snoozers in a 2024 survey ([Sundelin et al., Journal of Sleep Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jsr.14054)), and 7 among Japanese university students in a 2022 one, where 5 was the most common answer ([Ogawa et al., Journal of Physiological Anthropology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40101-022-00317-w)).

## How to change snooze time on iPhone

On iOS 26 and iOS 27, snooze length is set alarm by alarm in the Clock app, and Apple's guide gives the same steps for both versions ([iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/27/ios/27); [iOS 26](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/26/ios/26)):

1. Open Clock and tap Alarms.
2. Tap an alarm's time to edit it, or tap the Add button for a new alarm.
3. Turn on Snooze, the switch that gives the ringing alarm a Snooze button.
4. Tap Snooze Duration and choose how long the alarm pauses.
5. Tap Done.

Apple's guide does not list the choices. When iOS 26 introduced the setting, the picker ran from 1 to 15 minutes, 9 stayed the default, and each alarm kept its own length ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2025/09/30/ios-26-gave-your-iphones-alarm-brand-new-snooze-options/)). Check the picker on your own phone rather than trusting a number from last year.

If you wake with a sleep schedule from the Health app, its wake up alarm has its own snooze settings. In Clock, tap Alarms, then Change, scroll to Alarm Options, and set Snooze and Snooze Duration there. Tap Done, then Change Next Alarm Only for tomorrow, or Change This Schedule to update the recurring alarm ([Apple, iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-your-wake-up-alarm-iphf2a780f81/27/ios/27); [iOS 26](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-your-wake-up-alarm-iphf2a780f81/26/ios/26)). That split also explains one of the quieter causes in [why an iPhone alarm doesn't go off](/blog/iphone-alarm-didnt-go-off-causes). Snooze length lives in Clock; how loud the alarm comes back is a Settings question, covered in [iPhone alarm too quiet](/blog/iphone-alarm-volume-too-low).

### On iOS 18 and earlier, it is nine

On iOS 18 the only snooze option is the Snooze switch, and Apple's guide says it pauses the alarm for 9 minutes ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/set-an-alarm-iph2909d3a74/18/ios/18)). To see what you are running, open Settings, tap General, then About, and read iOS Version ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/109065)). iOS 26 and iOS 27 both run on iPhone 11 and later and on the second- and third-generation iPhone SE ([iOS 27](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/iphone-models-compatible-with-ios-27-iphe3fa5df43/ios); [iOS 26](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/iphone-models-compatible-with-ios-26-iphe3fa5df43/26/ios/26)), so on an older model, nine is what you get.

## How do I change snooze time on Android?

It depends on whose Clock app your phone runs, and the defaults differ before you touch anything.

- **Google's Clock app** (on Pixel phones and Google Play): tapping Snooze delays an alarm for 10 minutes ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2840926?hl=en)). To change it, open Clock, tap More, then Settings, and under Alarms tap Snooze length ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2841106?hl=en)). It sits in the app's settings rather than inside each alarm, and the same list can make the volume buttons snooze or dismiss an alarm.
- **Samsung's Clock app** (Galaxy phones): snooze is set inside each alarm. Open the alarm, tap Snooze, and set the interval and how many times it repeats, or tap Custom to set your own ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/us/support/answer/ANS10002623/)). Samsung's default is 5 minutes, repeated 3 times ([Samsung](https://www.samsung.com/ca/support/mobile-devices/set-and-manage-alarms-on-your-samsung-galaxy/)).
- **Other brands** have their own clock apps: look inside the alarm first, then in the app's settings.

So iPhone, Google and Samsung start you at 9, 10 and 5 minutes. Nobody agreed in 1959 either.

## What a snooze of any length does to your morning

Changing the number is easy now. Whether it matters is a harder question.

The best-controlled test, a 2024 lab study by Sundelin and colleagues, compared 30 minutes of snoozing with a single abrupt alarm in the same 31 habitual snoozers. Snoozing cost about six minutes of sleep, did not hurt performance on cognitive tests straight after rising, and kept people from being woken out of deep sleep ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37849039/)). The rest of the case for and against the button is in [why the snooze button feels so good](/blog/snooze-button-psychology-cost).

The Japanese study points the other way, and it tested short gaps: ten students slept in a lab with and without a phone snooze that sounded four times at 5-minute intervals across their last 20 minutes of sleep ([Ogawa et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40101-022-00317-w)). With the snoozes, those 20 minutes held 4 fewer minutes of sleep and more wakefulness and N1, the lightest stage. Afterwards the students reacted more slowly in the last of three reaction-time tests and rated themselves less vigorous, and the authors concluded that snoozing prolonged the grogginess. Ten people is a tiny sample, and the paper says so.

That leaves two lab studies, two snooze patterns and two conclusions, and nothing we could find that compares lengths head to head. Sundelin's team note that they tested only 30 minutes, and that shorter or longer snoozes may act differently. The consistent thread is what the minutes are made of. Deep sleep comes in longer stretches during the first half of the night ([NINDS](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/public-education/brain-basics/brain-basics-understanding-sleep)); in Sundelin's study, snoozing prevented awakenings from it, and in Ogawa's, nobody was woken from it, with or without snoozes. Whatever the legend says about engineers guarding you from the deep end, a snooze is mostly spent in the shallows.

## Choosing a snooze length on purpose

### 1. Do the arithmetic once

Presses times length is your real wake-up time. Three presses at nine minutes means the alarm you set is 27 minutes early, every day, and those 27 minutes are mostly light, broken sleep. If that is your routine, set one alarm for the time you actually get up, and plan the night backward from it with the [sleep calculator](/tools/sleep-calculator).

### 2. If you snooze, try fewer, longer gaps

Ogawa's team blamed repeated forced awakenings: their snoozers were roused about four times in those 20 minutes, against fewer than once without snoozes. A 10-minute snooze would ring half as often in the same window. That is an inference, not a tested rule, but a week of trying it costs nothing.

### 3. Give each alarm its own length

On iOS 26 and later, and in Samsung's Clock, every alarm keeps its own snooze: a short one where minutes are tight, a longer one on a slow Sunday. In Google's Clock the length is a single app setting, so pick the one your hardest morning needs.

### 4. Stuck at nine? Build your own gap

On iOS 18 and earlier, switch Snooze off and set a second alarm at the gap you want: 6:30 and 6:35, say. In Google's Clock, keep a ladder of alarms at least 5 minutes apart: Google warns that if two alarms are too close together, the first may be canceled ([Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/2840926?hl=en)).

### 5. If no length works, look past the button

Snoozers report shorter sleep and more morning drowsiness than people who never snooze ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37849039/)), so a daily pile of snoozes is often a bedtime problem wearing an alarm costume. The NHS advises seeing a doctor if you often fall asleep during the day or sleepiness is affecting your life, and lists loud snorting and snoring at night among the signs that can point to sleep apnoea ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/excessive-daytime-sleepiness-hypersomnia/)).

## FAQ

### Why is the iPhone alarm snooze 9 minutes?

Apple's guide for iOS 18 stated the nine minutes without giving a reason. The number is older than the iPhone: GE-Telechron's first snooze clock, in 1956, ran about 15 minutes before the company settled on a mechanism of around nine, while Westclox clocks used 5, 7 and 10. The popular gear-teeth explanation is a legend, and we could find no document behind it.

### Can you change snooze time on iPhone?

Yes, on iOS 26 and iOS 27. In Clock, tap Alarms, tap the alarm's time, then tap Snooze Duration and pick a length; each alarm keeps its own. On iOS 18 and earlier you cannot: snooze is fixed at nine minutes, so turn it off and set a second alarm at the gap you want.

### How do I get my iPhone alarm to snooze?

Open Clock, tap Alarms, tap the alarm's time and turn on Snooze; Apple describes it as the switch that shows a Snooze button with your alarm. If a ringing alarm has no Snooze button, the switch is off for that alarm. A sleep schedule's wake up alarm has its own switch, under Change, then Alarm Options.

### Is snoozing bad for you?

The evidence is small and split. The best-controlled lab study found that 30 minutes of snoozing cost about six minutes of sleep without hurting thinking on rising, while a ten-person study of four 5-minute snoozes found more grogginess afterwards. Heavy daily snoozing is more often a sign of short or late sleep than a harm in itself, and if you often fall asleep during the day, that is worth raising with a doctor.

## Nine was never sacred

Nine minutes was never a law of sleep. It was one company's revision of a quarter-hour snooze, then a phone's default, and now it is one row in an alarm editor. Pick the number on purpose, do the multiplication once, and spend the rest of the decision where it pays: on when you go to bed, and on what gets you out of it.
