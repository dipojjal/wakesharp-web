## What a walking alarm clock asks of you

Snoozing is [a fast, automatic response to a cue](/blog/snooze-button-psychology-cost), and it happens from the pillow. A walking alarm moves the task somewhere the pillow can't follow. Walking needs balance, a sense of where you are and somewhere to go, which is a good deal more of you than a thumb.

WakeSharp's movement missions:

- **Walk It Off**: take real steps, counted by your phone.
- **First Light**: walk to a window and hold your phone in the light.

Does moving early help with the grogginess itself? It seems to help how awake you feel more than how well you perform, which is a smaller thing. [The first 30 minutes after waking](/blog/first-30-minutes-brain-after-waking) has the detail, and the caveats.

## How the walking alarm works

1. **The night before**, choose Walk It Off, First Light, or both in a row, in the order you choose. Walk It Off followed by First Light makes a walk that ends at a window.
2. **When the alarm rings**, get up. For Walk It Off, walk with your phone. The route is up to you, so make it end somewhere useful, like the kettle. For First Light, head to a window and hold the phone in the light. Opening the curtains on the way is encouraged.
3. **However you quiet the alarm, the morning only counts once the mission is done.**
4. **If a mission can't run that morning**, say on a phone with no step counter, WakeSharp falls back to one that can.
5. **Afterward**, an optional two-minute warm-up plays three of the five brain games on a rotation and produces your Sharpness Score. The score comes from the warm-up, not from the walk.

## Who it suits

- **People who snooze from the pillow.** If the button wins most mornings, a walk asks for more than the reflex that presses it.
- **Weekend anchors.** Keeping Saturday's wake time close to Monday's is one of the simplest ways to shrink [the Monday tax on a weekend sleep-in](/blog/social-jet-lag-monday-tax), and one of the hardest to manage from under warm covers.
- **Heavy sleepers** who need the alarm to involve their legs, not just a thumb.
- **People whose phone charges on the nightstand.** You will be walking anyway, so the charger might as well live across the room, where the phone can't keep you company at midnight either.

## What it can't do

Steps get you upright, not sharp. No mission, this one included, is proven to shorten sleep inertia, and nothing stops you walking straight back to bed. If that is the risk, follow the walk with a [puzzle](/features/puzzle-alarm-clock) or some [arithmetic](/features/math-alarm-clock).

Walking half asleep in the dark is how toes find furniture, so clear the path the night before and turn a light on before you set off.

Your phone's own controls always work: nothing stops you switching the phone off. And no app can ring on a phone that is switched off or out of battery, so for anything you can't afford to miss, set a second alarm on another device.
